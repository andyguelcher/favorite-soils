## How it formed

![Katherine Kovich Evolutionary Diagram](evolution-diagram.jpg)

1. Colluvium was deposited aided by water and gravity

2. First volcanic eruption occurs depositing tephra, volcanic glass, and pumice

3. Second volcanic eruption occurs depositing more tephra, volcanic glass, and pumice

4. Vegetation grows and accumulates into the Oi horizon


## Annual Water Budget: A Comparison Between the Mountwow and Wauseon Soil Series

The water budget for the Wauseon soil series begins in a surplus with the precipitation averaging about 55mm and evapotranspiration around 4mm in January. The water budget goes into a use phase in July where the evapotranspiration becomes greater than the monthly precipitation with 116mm of evapotranspiration, while only 102mm of precipitation for the month. Wauseon then goes into a recharge phase from August through October where precipitation surpassed evapotranspiration again. This trend continues even more so in November and December when the water budget crosses into a surplus once again. The water budget for Mountwow has a similar pattern to the Wauseon series. The main difference was Mountwow has a much longer period in the surplus zone compared to Wauseon. The water budget for Mountwow begins in a surplus zone with 370mm of precipitation and about 4mm of evapotranspiration. This period of surplus lasts until June where it enters a short recharge phase, with only a small difference between precipitation and evapotranspiration. The soil series transitions into a use phase in July, similar to Wauseon, where evapotranspiration is 55mm and precipitation is only 40mm. This again, only lasts for one month until a recharge phase is reached from August to September, and precipitation overcomes evapotranspiration. Similar to the Wauseon series, a period of surplus follows. However, Mountwow has a longer period of surplus at the end of the year, lasting from September to December where precipitation reaches up to 380mm in November from the large amounts of snowfall on the volcano. 

## Plant Availabe Water Comparison between Mountwow and Wauseon

The plant available water for the Wauseon soil series is 11.8cm in the root zone. This total is 16.6% PAW across 71cm of roots. The plant available water for the Mountwow soil series is 30.5cm in the root zone. This total is 20.61% across 150cm of roots. The main difference between the two series is the depth that each root zone extends to. The measured Wauseon series extends to 200cm below the surface, but the root zone only extends down to 71cm, less than half of the total series length. The measured Mountwow series extends to 150cm below the surface and contains roots in all horizons. I believe this is due to the fact that there are multiple buried horizons in this series with lots of thick and diverse vegetation cover on the surface. Even though the two series have different plant available water lengths, it is distributed over different soil depths, which makes the total % plant available water between the two series so close, Wauseon’s 16.6% PAW compared to Mountwow’s 20.61%.
