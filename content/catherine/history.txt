The history of this soil, played by the "Play its history" button on the 3D model. Each stage starts with "Stage:". Deposit lists horizons laid down at that stage, Develop lists horizons that start to form, and Finish lists horizons that reach how they look today. Researched for the timeline by Claude (Anthropic's AI assistant), 12 Oct. 2026; full citations are in Sources on this page.

Stage: Glaciers retreat and colluvium slides in
When: after about 15,000 years ago, and before 7,630 years ago
Age: 12000
Deposit: 2Agb2, 2Bgb4, 2Bgb5
Text: Valley glaciers shrink back up Mount Rainier after the Evans Creek glaciation. Andesite gravel and cobbles slide and wash down the slopes of the Cowlitz Divide as colluvium, the first parent material.
Source: Mountwow OSD; Crandell 1969 (USGS Bulletin 1288)

Stage: A soil forms on the colluvium
When: before 7,630 years ago
Age: 9500
Develop: 2Agb2, 2Bgb4, 2Bgb5
Text: Plants grow on the colluvium and an A horizon darkens at its surface. That old surface is the lithologic discontinuity at 66 cm today.
Source: Mountwow OSD

Stage: Mount Mazama erupts (ash layer O)
When: about 7,630 years ago
Age: 7630
Deposit: Bgb3
Text: Mount Mazama in Oregon erupts and collapses to form Crater Lake. Its ash falls across the Pacific Northwest and buries the first soil.
Source: Jensen and others 2019; Tephra Explorer (NPS); Mountwow OSD

Stage: Mount Rainier erupts (layer D)
When: roughly 7,000 years ago
Age: 7000
Deposit: Bgb2
Text: Mount Rainier itself adds tephra layer D on top of the Mazama ash.
Source: Vallance and Scott (Osceola Mudflow); Mountwow OSD

Stage: Mount Rainier erupts (layer F)
When: about 5,600 years ago
Age: 5600
Deposit: Bgb1
Text: Around the time its summit collapses into the Osceola Mudflow, Mount Rainier erupts layer F.
Source: Vallance and Scott (Osceola Mudflow); Mountwow OSD

Stage: A meadow soil forms on the ash
When: about 5,600 to 3,660 years ago
Age: 4600
Deposit: Agb1
Develop: Agb1, Bgb1, Bgb2, Bgb3, 2Agb2
Text: For nearly 2,000 years no large ash falls here. The meadow builds a new dark A horizon on top of the ash, and the ash beneath begins to weather.
Source: Mountwow OSD

Stage: Mount St. Helens erupts (layer Yn)
When: about 3,660 years ago
Age: 3660
Deposit: Bw3
Text: A large eruption of Mount St. Helens drops layer Yn and buries the meadow soil, which survives today as the buried Agb horizon.
Source: Jensen and others 2019; Mountwow OSD

Stage: Mount St. Helens erupts (layer P)
When: about 3,000 to 2,550 years ago
Age: 2800
Deposit: Bw2
Text: Mount St. Helens adds the layers of tephra set P.
Source: USGS tephra framework; Mountwow OSD

Stage: Mount Rainier erupts (layer C)
When: about 2,200 years ago
Age: 2200
Deposit: Bw1
Text: Mount Rainier erupts tephra layer C.
Source: Sisson and Vallance 2009; Mountwow OSD

Stage: Mount St. Helens erupts (layer Wn)
When: 1480 CE, about 550 years ago
Age: 546
Deposit: A
Text: Mount St. Helens drops layer Wn, the youngest ash in the profile. Today's A horizon forms in it.
Source: Tephra Explorer (NPS); Mountwow OSD

Stage: Today
When: every year
Age: 1
Deposit: Oi
Finish: all
Text: Snowmelt saturates the soil each spring, leaving grey depletions and reddish iron along root channels. The weathering ash gives the soil its andic properties, and heather, huckleberry and lupine add a thin Oi of plant litter.
Source: Mountwow OSD
